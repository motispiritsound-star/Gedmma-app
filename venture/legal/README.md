# Policy drafts

**These are drafts for a lawyer to review, not publishable policies.**

Every document in this directory was written from secondary-source research. No
primary legal text could be opened from the build environment — EUR-Lex,
business.gov.nl, the NVWA and every law-firm source were blocked by the network
policy (`../ACCESS-AND-LIMITS.md`). Compliance vendors also have a commercial
interest in overstating obligations, and several of the underlying sources sell
the solution to the problem they describe.

So treat these as a **well-informed starting point that saves a lawyer time**,
not as advice and not as finished text.

## What must be filled in before any of this is published

Placeholders appear as `[SQUARE BRACKETS]`. The store cannot lawfully trade
without them, because EU distance-selling rules require the trader to be
identifiable:

- `[LEGAL ENTITY NAME]`, `[REGISTERED ADDRESS]`
- `[KVK NUMBER]` — Dutch Chamber of Commerce registration
- `[BTW NUMBER]` — Dutch VAT identification
- `[SUPPORT EMAIL]`, `[SUPPORT RESPONSE TIME]`
- `[RETURN ADDRESS]` — must be a real address that accepts returns

## What a lawyer must specifically confirm

| # | Item | Why it is uncertain |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The withdrawal-button obligation from 19 June 2026** | Reported by several sources to apply horizontally to goods, but it sits in a directive about financial services. The research flagged this as the single most commonly misread item it encountered |
| 2 | **Who bears return postage, and whether our wording secures that** | The consumer bears it only if told in advance, in the right form. Get this wrong and we pay every return |
| 3 | **GPSR responsible-person status** | Whether the entity is importer, manufacturer or both per product line |
| 4 | **VAT and OSS registration timing** | When distance sales trigger registration, and in which scheme |
| 5 | **EPR registration thresholds** | NL packaging appears to have a 50 t/yr threshold; the single-use-plastic surcharge reportedly does not |
| 6 | **Whether the guarantee wording creates obligations beyond the statutory two years** | A commercial guarantee is enforceable as written |

## What these drafts deliberately do NOT do

They do not promise anything the business cannot honour, and they do not dress
statutory rights up as generosity. The EU two-year conformity guarantee exists
whether or not we mention it; presenting it as a favour is both misleading and,
under the Omnibus Directive, a potential unfair commercial practice.

Nor do they contain a "satisfaction guarantee" or a returns window longer than
operations can actually service. A published promise is a contractual one.
