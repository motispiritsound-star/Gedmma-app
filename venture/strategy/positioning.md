# Positioning and brand

Written 2026-09-12.

## The customer

Not "padel players". A padel player in Marbella has no problem to solve.

**The recreational-to-club player in Northwest Europe who plays outdoors, all
year, in weather.** They play one to three times a week, own a mid-range racket
they chose carefully, and carry their kit to a club rather than keeping it at
home. They are 28–50, and they have already demonstrated willingness to spend on
the sport.

The specific, recurring, unglamorous problem: **kit gets wet, and wet kit gets
worse.** Soaked grips are unusable. Damp shoes and towels sit in the same
compartment as dry clothes. Bags get thrown down on wet courts and into wet car
boots. Nothing in the category is designed for this, because the category was
designed in Spain.

## The positioning statement

> For players who play outdoors in Northern European weather, **[BRAND]** makes
> padel kit engineered for wet conditions — bags that keep wet and dry apart,
> grips that work in the rain, and a subscription that means you never play on a
> worn grip again. Designed and tested in the Netherlands, shipped from the
> Netherlands, with duties and VAT included and nothing to pay at the door.

## Why this is defensible and not just a slogan

The category's established brands — Bullpadel, Nox, Head, Babolat — are Spanish,
French and Latin American in origin, and their product assumptions follow their
climate. That is not a criticism; it is a gap. A brand that treats rain as the
normal case rather than the exception is making a genuinely different product,
not a differently-branded one.

Supporting layers, honestly ranked (see `business-case.md` §7): GPSR compliance
as an EU-established seller is durable against non-EU competitors and worthless
against EU ones; the climatic design lead is a 12–18 month head start rather than
a moat; delivery and duty certainty is eroding as Temu and Shein move to EU
warehouses; club and player community is the slowest to build and the hardest to
copy.

## Reasons to believe

Each of these must be **true and demonstrable before it is claimed**. None may
appear on the storefront until it can be substantiated — unsubstantiated claims
are both an Omnibus Directive problem and a GPSR problem.

- Wet/dry separation as a physical compartment, shown in a photograph
- A stated hydrostatic-head figure for the outer fabric, from the mill's spec
- PFAS-free durable water repellent, evidenced by supplier declaration
- Ships from the Netherlands; a stated delivery date range, not a duration
- A named, numbered guarantee with published exclusions

## Naming

The strongest candidate is **POLDER**.

A polder is land reclaimed from water and kept dry by engineering. The Dutch are
the world's acknowledged experts at keeping water out, and that is precisely what
the product does. The name ties nationality to product benefit rather than merely
asserting heritage — the rare case where a national story is an argument rather
than decoration.

It is two syllables, pronounceable in Dutch, English, German and French, spelled
the same in all of them, and category-flexible if the brand later extends beyond
padel into other outdoor-sport weather protection.

| Alternative | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| **KADE** (quay) | Short, Dutch, clean | Meaning is opaque outside NL |
| **LUWTE** (the sheltered side) | Beautiful and exactly on-message | Most non-Dutch speakers cannot pronounce it |
| **DROOG** (dry) | The most literally on-message name available | **Likely conflict** with Droog, the well-known Dutch design house. Recommend against |
| **TEGENWIND** (headwind) | Evocative, sporting | Long, and hard for non-Dutch speakers |

## Tone of voice

Plain, specific, slightly dry. Dutch directness is an asset: state the number,
state the exclusion, do not oversell. The category's incumbent tone is Spanish
sporting passion; the contrast is the point.

- **Do:** "Hydrostatic head 5,000 mm. It will keep out Dutch rain. It is not a
  dry bag and will not survive submersion."
- **Don't:** "Unleash your game in any conditions."

Concretely: numbers over adjectives, exclusions stated alongside promises, no
performance claims about the player's game, no health or injury claims of any
kind.

## Visual identity direction

Not a competitor's identity, and deliberately not the category's.

The padel category defaults to high-saturation neon, aggressive diagonals and
action photography — a visual language borrowed from tennis and football. The
counter-position is **technical, calm and Northern**: a restrained palette built
from wet-weather greys, deep water blue and a single high-visibility accent for
calls to action; generous whitespace; photography in actual grey Dutch weather
rather than studio-lit sunshine; product shots that show water on fabric.

Typography: one grotesque for everything, two weights only, for performance
(§ theme budget) and for the plain tone.

**Photography is the single largest asset gap.** Every image on the storefront
must be original — supplier images carry unclear commercial rights and would
also, in this category, show the wrong weather. See `marketing/` for the asset
plan.
