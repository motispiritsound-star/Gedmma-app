The path

Seventeen units, each built on the one before. Below, what is in each unit and the notes the app gives along the way — the grammar, in one place.

🔤 Lhruf

A0 الحروف
The Arabic alphabet, letter by letter — ا ب ت ث · ج ح خ د ذ · ر ز س ش · ص ض ط ظ · ع غ ف ق · ك ل م ن · ه و ي · Checkpoint
Right to left

Arabic is read and written from right to left. Letters join up, so the same letter looks different at the start, in the middle and at the end of a word. ب، ت and ث are exactly the same stroke — only the dots differ.

Six letters never join forward

ا د ذ ر ز و never join to the letter after them. That is why a word sometimes breaks apart in the middle without being two words. Every other letter sticks on both sides.

The heavy letters

ص ض ط ظ are the dark cousins of س د ت ز. You make them with your tongue flat and far back, as if you were talking with your mouth full. You can hear the difference: سيف (sword) and صيف (summer).

The most famous sound

ع is the sound Arabic is famous for: a squeeze deep in your throat. In messages Moroccans write a 3 for it, because a 3 looks like ع. غ is the rasping French r, and ق a k right at the back.

The last three

That is all twenty-eight letters. و and ي are the two that are sometimes a consonant (w, y) and sometimes a long vowel (oo, ee) — the word tells you which.

👋 Salam!

A0 السلام
Saying hello and goodbye — Hello and goodbye · How are you? · Yes, no, okay · A fond goodbye · Checkpoint
What is Darija?

Darija is the language of the street in Morocco. It looks like Arabic, but with its own words from Amazigh, French and Spanish. Here you learn both the Arabic script and the letters Moroccans use in their messages.

Boy or girl?

Darija changes the word when you speak to a girl: kif dayr? to a boy, kif dayra? to a girl. You will meet that -a everywhere.

The numbers inside the words

In messages Moroccans write 3 for ع, 7 for ح and 9 (or q) for ق. Those sounds do not exist in English. 3afak = please, 7amdullah = thank God.

🙋 Ana w nta

A0 انا ونتا
Me, you, and who you are — Me and you · What is your name? · Where are you from? · Checkpoint
No “to be”

Darija has no word for “am”. “Ana ferhan” is literally “I happy” and means “I am happy”. That saves a lot of work.

👨‍👩‍👧‍👦 L3a'ila

A0 العائلة
Introducing your family — At home · Uncles and aunts · This is my… · Checkpoint
Two kinds of uncle

Your father's family (3ammi, 3ammti) and your mother's (khali, khalti) have their own words. Moroccans ask straight away: which side?

The -i of “my”

Stick -i on a word and it is yours: khoya (my brother), sahbi (my friend), smiti (my name). Stick -ek on and it is the other person's: smitek (your name).

🔢 L3dad

A0 الأعداد
Counting to a hundred — One to five · Six to ten · Counting further · How old are you? · Checkpoint
Two is “jouj”

For 2 Darija uses jouj (from Arabic zawj, “pair”), not tnayn. Jouj drari = two children.

🎨 Lalwan

A0 الألوان
The colours around you — The six basic colours · More colours · Checkpoint
Colours take an -a too

A red car is tomobil hemra, a red book is ktab hmer. The word adapts to the thing you are describing.

🍽️ Lmakla

A1 الماكلة
Food, drink and tea — On the table · Fruit and vegetables · From the fridge · Moroccan dishes · I am hungry · Checkpoint
Atay is a ritual

Mint tea is poured from high up so it foams. Three glasses is the custom — refusing is almost rude.

Friday is couscous day

After the Friday prayer almost all of Morocco eats kesksu, from one big dish in the middle of the table.

A1 الوقت

The week starts on Sunday

lhedd (Sunday) comes from “one”, lethnin (Monday) from “two”, ttlat from “three”. The days are simply counted.

💛 Ljism

A1 الجسم
Body and feelings — Your face · Your body · How do you feel? · Checkpoint
Kanbghik

Kanbghik means both “I love you” and “I like you”. You say it to your mother, your grandmother and your best friend.

⚡ Kanhder

A1 كنهضر
Saying what you do — Every day · More things to do · Wanting and having · Do it! · Checkpoint
The ka- of “now”

Put ka(n)- in front of a verb and it happens now or always: nakol (that I eat) becomes kanakol (I eat, I am eating).

Giving an order

An order is the bare verb: sir (go), aji (come), shuf (look). To a girl an -i is added, to several people a -u: siri, siru.

❓ Su'al

A1 السؤال
Asking questions — The question words · Questions you really use · Checkpoint
The question word may stay at the end

Unlike English, the sentence does not move: “smitek shnu?” (your name what?) works just as well as “shnu smitek?”.

🛍️ Fssouq

A2 السوق
At the market — Where and with what · Haggling · Big or small · Checkpoint
Haggling is part of it

At the souq the first price is an opening bid. Kindly name about half, smile, and meet in the middle.

🇲🇦 Lmghrib dyalna

A2 المغرب ديالنا
Country, cities and celebration — The country and its languages · Celebration and tradition · Checkpoint
Two official languages

Morocco has Arabic and Tamazight (Amazigh) as official languages. Tamazight has its own alphabet, Tifinagh: ⵜⵉⴼⵉⵏⴰⵖ. Darija is what people speak at home.
